RealMe Studio

1 · Draft the narration

Nothing is synthesized in this step, so it costs one cheap model call. The two context boxes matter more than the model you pick — they are what makes the script sound like you rather than like a textbook.

Where an ordinary slide should land. A section divider or title slide should get far less — ten or twenty words is a finished title slide — and a dense slide may run to the ceiling (160 / 256 / 350 words).

Logos and template art are found in the deck and the model is told to pass over them — a mark that repeats in the same spot on most pages, or a small one out at the edge, is branding rather than content. Without that it writes a sentence about the magnifying glass. Real figures, charts and diagrams are still narrated, and a section divider carrying nothing but a logo is no longer mistaken for one.

Each slide then gets its own band, measured from the slide: a section divider is owed nothing, a simple one-claim slide finishes comfortably under a hundred words, a dense table or derivation is owed more than the setting's middle, and a full-page figure is owed real narration however little text it carries — the picture does not read itself.

The draft is measured against those bands, not just asked for them, because a model settles at about a hundred words a slide whatever you request. Slides that came in under their own band are sent back once with their words shown to them — asked for the step that was skipped or the number never read out, never for padding, and a rewrite that came back no longer is discarded. The log says which slides moved and what each was owed.

…or bring your own notes

Already written the narration? Import it instead of having a model write it — no API key needed, nothing rewritten. One text file for the whole deck, with a line marking each slide:

[[slide 1: Why confounding is structural]]
Confounding is not a statistical problem that appears at the analysis stage…

[[slide 2]]
Consider a cohort where 15.3 percent received the intervention…

## Slide 1 and ## Slide 1 - Title work too. Slide numbers must run 1…N with none skipped or repeated: a script that does not is refused rather than mapped onto the wrong slides.

…or reopen a lecture to read or re-record it

The notes you already approved are saved with the project. Reopen it to read them, edit them, or record the whole thing again — after changing the voice, the pause scale, the layout, or nothing at all. No model call, and no need to import anything: re-rendering reads the script that is already there.

Speech whose words, voice and pace have not changed is reused from the ledger, so a re-record is usually a few minutes rather than the original hour. Record every utterance again ignores that and speaks the whole lecture from scratch — the way to get a different take from the same words.

…or revise a lecture you have already made

Changed a figure, corrected a definition, inserted two slides? Upload the new deck against the project you rendered before. Slides are matched by what they SAY — not by position, and not by pixels — so re-exporting the deck with a different theme moves no notes, and inserting one slide at the front does not make the other forty count as new. Narration and cue words follow their slide, and each one is marked in the editor below as new, edited, moved or unchanged.

Read a text aloud

      
No deck, no script writer, no model call — text in, your voice
        out. This is the loop for checking a pronunciation, hearing how a paragraph
        lands, or testing a new reference clip. Blank lines separate paragraphs and
        get a longer breath; [[pause]], [[slow]] and
        [[emphasis]]…[[/emphasis]] work exactly as they do in a lecture.

      

      
      
Select words first and a delivery marker wraps them; with
        nothing selected it applies from that point to the end of the paragraph.
        Markers are instructions to the engine — they are never spoken, and they
        do not appear in the captions.

      
      

        
        
        
      

      

        
        
        
        
      

      
Expression only affects the cloning engines — the draft voice
        does not sample. Pace is deterministic: it is the right control for speed,
        unlike expression, which changes speed only as a side effect.

Split a video into parts

      
A long lecture cut into sections, at exact slide
        boundaries. Every render writes a cut list beside the video with each
        slide's measured start time, so "begin part two at slide 21" is a
        lookup rather than a hunt for the right frame. Only videos RealMe
        rendered appear here; without that cut list there is nothing that says
        where a slide begins.

      

        
        
      

      

      

      
      

        
        
      

    

    


    

      
Join videos into one

      
The other direction: several videos played end to end.
        Where every input shares the same size, codec and frame rate — which is
        true of parts cut from one lecture — they are joined without
        re-encoding, so nothing is lost and it takes seconds. Where they
        differ, they are re-encoded to match, because copying mismatched files
        produces something that plays the first video and then goes black.

      
The inputs may come from different projects. Two sections
        taken from two lectures become a new lecture: the narration, the
        cue words and the slide images follow their video into the new order, a
        deck is built from them, and the result opens in the Lecture tab where
        you can fix a sentence and record it again. Captions are merged and
        shifted to follow, and the joined video gets its own cut list, so it can
        be split again.

      

        
        
      

      
      
      

      

        
        
      

      

      

      

        
      

      

        
        
      

    

    


    

      
Pronunciations

      
A term on the left, how it should sound on the right,
        written in ordinary letters — en-haynes,
        why-oh-uh. Every engine understands a
        respelling, which is why it is the column that matters.

      
Leave the pronunciation blank to stop a term being
        spelled out letter by letter without claiming to know how it sounds.
        That is the right entry for something like NHANES, which the engine
        already says correctly but the acronym speller would otherwise turn
        into “N H A N E S”.

      

        
        
        
      

      

      

      

    

  


  

    

      
How this works

      
Two voices and one rule. Piper is the draft voice —
        fast, not yours, for getting the words and the slide timing right.
        qwen3cpp is your own cloned voice and records the final audio on
        this machine; nothing about your voice leaves it. The rule: iterate with
        the draft, record once with your own.

      
Three voices now, and the rule has not changed — only
        which one records the final audio by default.

      

        
piperFree, local, not your voice. The draft
          voice, and the second speaker in a podcast. Iterate here: getting
          the words and the slide timing right costs nothing and takes
          minutes. You can use the whole system this way and never spend
          anything.

        
gemini-ttsYour voice, hosted, about a dollar an
          hour of audio. The default for a new lecture since October 2026:
          closer to you than the local clone, no GPU, minutes rather than
          hours, 130 languages. Your reference clip and every sentence you
          render are sent to Google, so it wants a paid project. The render
          log says what each slide cost as it goes.

        
qwen3cppYour voice, here, free. Nothing about
          your voice leaves the machine and it works with the network
          unplugged. Slower — about six times slower than real time on a
          laptop CPU — and it is the one that will still render this lecture
          identically in five years. Pick it in Twin Setup, or per render in
          the Lecture tab.

Editing the notes

      
Everything in the editor is free. Every word below it costs
        minutes of compute, so fix things here first.

      
    
        
  • Say the numbers you mean. "15.3 percent" is read as one
          number; "15.3%" is too, but writing the word is safer in a sentence
          that also uses a percent sign for something else.
    • 
        
  • Acronyms are spelled out by default. AUC becomes
          "A U C". A term that is genuinely a word — NHANES, NASA — needs a
          lexicon entry: realme lexicon add NHANES --respelling
          "en-haynes", or --no-spell to stop it being
          spelled without asserting a pronunciation.
    • 
        
  • LaTeX is converted, and you can check it before rendering:
          realme math '\hat{\beta}_1 \pm 1.96\,\mathrm{SE}'.
    • 
        
  • Cue words sit under each slide's notes, semicolons between
          them. Each must appear both on the slide and in that slide's narration
          — the render says which one failed rather than quietly showing
          nothing.
    • 
        
  • Hear one line before committing to a whole render. The button
          under each slide synthesizes the first utterance in seconds.
    • 
      

    


    

      
Delivery markers

      
Instructions to the engine, written inline. They are never
        spoken and never appear in the captions, and they work in slide notes,
        narration text and podcast scripts alike. The Narrate tab has a button
        for each.

      

        
[[pause]]Silence. 350 ms by default;
          [[pause:900]] for longer, 2 s maximum.

        
[[breath]]A 180 ms catch of breath.

        
[[hedging]]Prefixes the next phrase with a
          spoken "erh…".

        
[[mask]]A 450 ms tone instead of the words.
          [[mask]]…[[/mask]] masks a span.

        
[[unmask]]Ends a masked span; the same as
          [[/mask]].

        
[[slow]] [[fast]]8% slower or
          quicker.

        
[[soft]] [[clear]]Quieter, or
          especially deliberate articulation.

        
[[emphasis]]…[[/emphasis]]Modest stress on
          the marked phrase.

        
[[normal]]Closes every delivery marker still
          open.

      

      
The delivery markers are scoped: wrap a phrase with a
        closing tag, or write one on its own and it runs to the end of the
        paragraph. A mistyped marker is reported before anything is synthesized —
        you cannot hear the difference between a marker that worked and one that
        was ignored.

Recording the slides

      
    
        
  • Draft first. Pick a draft voice in step 2 and render — minutes
          instead of an hour, saved as <project>_draft.mp4 so it
          cannot overwrite a finished recording. Check the words, the pacing and
          the slide timing, then re-render in your own voice.
    • 
        
  • Expect roughly 3–5 minutes of compute per minute of audio for
          your own voice on a CPU. A twenty-minute lecture is about an hour. It
          runs unattended.
    • 
        
  • Narration length: a band for ordinary slides, and a ceiling
          nobody has to reach. Brief, Standard and Full say where a slide of
          ordinary weight should land — about 50–70, 100–150 or 160–200 words.
          A section divider or title slide should fall far below its band; ten
          words can be a finished title slide. A dense slide may run up to the
          ceiling (160, 256 or 350 words). If every slide comes out the same
          length, or the deck's median sits well under its band, the draft says
          so — the first is narration written to a quota, the second means the
          setting never reached the model.
    • 
        
  • Revising later is cheap. Upload a new version of the deck
          against the same project: slides are matched by what they say, so a
          re-theme moves no notes and inserting one slide does not re-record
          the other forty.
    • 
        
  • A comparison is a proposal until you save it. Comparing a
          new deck against a project changes nothing on disk: your old deck,
          its slides and its notes all stay where they are, and the new deck
          waits in the project's working folder. The editor shows the proposed
          result with a banner above it. Save and apply is the moment
          the new deck becomes the project's deck; Discard this revision
          leaves everything exactly as it was. Rendering is refused while a
          proposal is open, rather than guessing which deck you meant.
    • 
        
  • "Edited" is exact, and it shows its working. Whether the
          wording changed is decided by comparing the words themselves, not by
          a similarity score — a score cannot tell six changed words on a long
          slide from none, and once reported such a slide as unchanged. Each
          edited slide carries the change underneath its badge
          ("6 words added, 1 removed: added “and figures”"), so a
          flag you disagree with can be dismissed on sight. A difference that
          is only the printed page number is not an edit.
    • 
        
  • New slides can be drafted into the lecture you already have.
          After a comparison, Draft notes for the new slides sends only
          the slides marked NEW to the model, together with the narration on
          either side of them, so what comes back picks up from the previous
          slide instead of opening a lecture that is already half over. Slides
          marked EDITED keep the notes you wrote; where one genuinely needs a
          change, ✎ Update these notes on that slide asks for the
          smallest edit that fits it and leaves the rest of your wording alone.
          Both are drafts — read them before rendering.
    • 
        
  • You decide what gets remade. Each slide carries a
          re-record this slide tick, set from what actually differs.
          A comparison cannot tell "the logo moved" from "the figure is wrong";
          untick the first and that slide keeps its existing frame and audio at
          no cost. Editing its narration overrides the tick — new words need new
          audio, so the slide is rendered whatever the box says, and the render
          log tells you it happened.
    •

Inside a project

      

        
_work/manifest.jsonThe notes you edit. Narration,
          cue words, slide mapping and measured durations. This is the file to
          back up.

        
<deck>.mp4The video, with subtitles muxed in as a
          selectable track.

        
<deck>.srtSubtitles for YouTube, Canvas, Panopto —
          the format nearly everything ingests.

        
<deck>.vttThe same captions for HTML5 video and web
          players.

        
<deck>_chapters.txtPer-slide timestamps. Paste into a
          YouTube description and it becomes a clickable chapter list. Rounded to
          whole seconds, which is right for navigation and wrong for cutting.

        
<deck>_segments.jsonExact slide boundaries to three
          decimals, for splicing a revised version later. Written for every
          render.

        
<deck>_cues.jsonWhere each highlight was drawn and
          when — for checking the signalling, not for publishing.

        
<deck>_verification.jsonDuration, drift, streams, the
          pronunciation check, and which voice made the file.

      

      
.srt and .vtt are the same captions
        in two dialects — use whichever your platform accepts. The chapters file
        is the one that makes a forty-minute lecture navigable.

    


    

      
When something is wrong

      
    
        
  • A voice that is not yours. Every run prints which voice it
          built — cloning from voice_reference.wav, or a warning that
          no reference is enrolled. Check it in the job log before a long render.
    • 
        
  • A word mispronounced. Add a lexicon entry and render again;
          only the changed lines are re-synthesized.
    • 
        
  • Anything else. realme doctor builds every engine
          the way a render builds it and reports what is missing, with the command
          that fixes each one.
    • 
      

      
Diagnostics for this machine are on the Twin Setup tab.

Gemini cloned voice not set up

      
This is the voice a new lecture uses. It is
        hosted: your clone lives in a Google project, and everything it says —
        along with the clip it was cloned from — leaves this computer. It is
        faster and closer to you than the local engine, it needs no GPU, and a
        fifty-minute lecture costs about a dollar.

      
The local engine (qwen3cpp) is still there and
        still works with the network unplugged, at no cost and no upload. Pick
        it under Voice for lectures above, or per render in the Lecture
        tab. If you were already using it, this update did not move you.

      
Before anything is uploaded. On Google's free
        tier, their API terms say human reviewers may read API input and
        output, that the content is used to improve Google products, and that
        you should not submit personal information — and your voice is
        personal information. On the paid tier they state the opposite: not
        used for improvement, logged only briefly for abuse detection. No API
        reports which tier a key is on, so this is the one question only you
        can answer.

      
      
Google requires this exact sentence, in your own voice.
        A paraphrase is rejected. It is a different recording from the consent
        clip above, which is in RealMe's wording and stays for your
        institution.

      
Record it on the same microphone, in the same room,
        as your voice reference — ideally in the same sitting. Google
        compares the two recordings and refuses the pair if its speaker check
        says they are different people. A good desk mic for one and a phone
        for the other fails that check even though both are plainly you: it is
        comparing the spectral envelope, and a different capsule at a
        different distance changes it more than two speakers' voices do. Aim
        for 10–30 seconds; read the sentence slowly, or twice.

Speaking pace. A cloned voice speaks at the pace
